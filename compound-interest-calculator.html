Calculate how money can grow through compound interest. Enter your starting balance, interest rate, time and compounding frequency, with optional regular contributions.

Compound interest formula

For an initial amount without additional contributions, the standard compound-interest formula is:

A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt) P = initial amount r = annual interest rate as a decimal n = number of compounding periods per year t = time in years A = final amount Interest earned = A − P

Example

Suppose you invest $1,000 at 7% annual interest, compounded monthly, for 10 years. P = 1,000 r = 0.07 n = 12 t = 10 A = 1,000 × (1 + 0.07/12)^(12 × 10) The calculator evaluates the formula and reports the final balance and interest earned.

Important note about regular contributions

When regular contributions are included, the calculator compounds the starting balance and then applies each contribution at the end of its selected contribution period. Actual financial products can use different contribution timing, fees, taxes, or compounding rules, so use your account's terms for exact projections.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is compound interest?

Compound interest means interest is added to the balance and can itself earn interest in later periods.

Does compounding frequency matter?

Yes. With the same nominal annual rate and other conditions, more frequent compounding generally changes the final balance because interest is credited more often.

Can I include monthly contributions?

Yes. Enter a regular contribution and choose its frequency. The calculator includes those contributions in the projected balance.
